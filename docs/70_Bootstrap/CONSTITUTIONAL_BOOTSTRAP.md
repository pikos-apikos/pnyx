# CONSTITUTIONAL_BOOTSTRAP

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the system may come into existence before it can fully justify itself through its own recursive civic loop.

Its purpose is to make the founding phase explicit, bounded, and auditable.

The constitutional bootstrap is not treated as ordinary governance.
It is a temporary legitimacy bridge used to establish the minimum conditions under which ordinary, governance, and constitutional processes may later become self-revising.

The bootstrap exists because no system can derive its first legitimacy conditions entirely from rules that do not yet exist.
The goal is therefore not to pretend that the founding moment is neutral, but to constrain it, expose it, and convert it into civic review as early as possible.

---

## 2. Founding Problem

Before recursive civic governance can operate, the system must already have:
- an initial values layer,
- initial scope limits,
- an initial classification regime,
- initial thresholds,
- an initial skill registry seed,
- an initial operator trust arrangement,
- an initial membership rule,
- an initial audit commitment.

These cannot be produced by the full system because the full system does not yet exist.

This creates a constitutional bootstrap problem:
who is permitted to define the first framework, and under what limits?

---

## 3. Founding Principle

The bootstrap phase is permitted only as a temporary constitutional exception whose sole legitimate purpose is to create the conditions for its own public review, limitation, and eventual displacement by the civic loop.

The bootstrap may found the system.
It may not permanently own the system.

---

## 4. Constitutional Bootstrap Debt

Every bootstrap arrangement creates constitutional debt.

Constitutional debt is the gap between:
- what has been imposed or seeded before full civic legitimacy exists, and
- what the public has later ratified, revised, or rejected through the governed process.

Bootstrap debt must be:
- named,
- measured,
- publicly logged,
- kept minimal,
- retired as early as possible.

A bootstrap that denies its own debt is presumptively capture-prone.

---

## 5. Bootstrap Scope

The constitutional bootstrap may define only the minimum viable constitutional substrate required to start the system.

This includes:
- the initial values declaration,
- the initial governance layers,
- the initial bootstrap-fixed parameters,
- the initial skill and operator admission constraints,
- the initial challenge rights,
- the initial audit and publication duties,
- the initial constitutional review schedule.

The bootstrap must not define broad substantive policy agendas beyond what is necessary to enable institutional startup.

Bootstrap power is enabling power, not normal governing power.

---

## 6. Minimum Founding Artifacts

A valid bootstrap must publish, at minimum:
- `GOVERNANCE.md`
- `ARCHITECTURE.md`
- `BOOTSTRAP_PARAMETERS.md`
- `INVARIANTS.md`
- `CLASSIFICATION.md`
- `OPERATOR_TRUST_MODEL.md`
- `SKILL_REGISTRY.md`
- an initial public values declaration
- an initial constitutional review calendar
- an initial exception register listing all known founding shortcuts

No constitutional bootstrap is legitimate if its foundational artifacts are partly hidden.

---

## 7. Bootstrap Roles

The bootstrap phase may include the following temporary roles:
- founders,
- constitutional stewards,
- bootstrap operators,
- initial reviewers,
- bootstrap auditors,
- public challengers.

These roles are temporary and legitimacy-limited.
They do not imply permanent constitutional standing.

No bootstrap role may automatically convert into a permanent privileged governance role without later civic authorization.

---

## 8. Founders

Founders are the persons or institutions that publish the initial constitutional substrate.

Founders may:
- draft the initial framework,
- declare initial assumptions,
- publish initial system boundaries,
- identify the initial unresolved questions,
- seed initial operational capacity.

Founders may not claim that their initial framework is self-legitimating merely because it is internally coherent.

The founder role is a disclosed source of constitutional asymmetry.
It is tolerated only because the asymmetry is explicit and reviewable.

---

## 9. Constitutional Stewards

Constitutional stewards are temporary custodians of the bootstrap framework until the civic loop becomes minimally functional.

Their duty is conservative, not creative.
They exist to:
- preserve the founding artifacts,
- prevent silent drift,
- maintain the bootstrap review schedule,
- surface unresolved legitimacy questions,
- initiate the first constitutional review path.

Stewards must not use bootstrap authority to insert unrelated constitutional content.

---

## 10. Bootstrap Operators

Bootstrap operators may run the runtime, deployments, registries, key material, and audit infrastructure required during the founding phase.

Bootstrap operators are governed by `OPERATOR_TRUST_MODEL.md` and inherit all prohibitions against hidden runtime concentration, silent override, and active-case retuning.

No bootstrap operator may simultaneously hold unrestricted power over:
- production deployment,
- skill registry admission,
- audit publication,
- classification override,
- emergency declaration.

---

## 11. Bootstrap Auditors

The bootstrap phase must include an audit function independent of founder authorship and operator control.

Bootstrap auditors must be able to inspect:
- founding artifacts,
- bootstrap parameter settings,
- operator actions,
- registry decisions,
- classification events,
- emergency invocations,
- first constitutional review preparation.

Bootstrap audit may be thin at first, but it may not be absent.

---

## 12. Bootstrap Public

The bootstrap phase must define who counts as the initial civic public for review, challenge, and eventual ratification.

The bootstrap public may be narrower than the final civic public, but this narrowing must be:
- justified,
- explicit,
- temporary,
- challengeable,
- reviewed in the first constitutional cycle.

A bootstrap that silently equates its founders with the public is invalid in spirit even if operationally functional.

---

## 13. Initial Values Declaration

The bootstrap must publish an initial values declaration.

This declaration may be provisional, but it must be concrete enough to govern early routing, anti-capture review, and rights-sensitive classification.

The declaration must distinguish between:
- core values claimed as load-bearing,
- aspirational values,
- unresolved value tensions,
- values whose prioritization is explicitly deferred to future civic review.

The bootstrap must not present contested value rankings as though they were objective necessities.

---

## 14. Value Tension Register

The bootstrap must publish a value tension register.

This register records known constitutional tensions such as:
- liberty vs. collective protection,
- subsidiarity vs. universality,
- transparency vs. privacy,
- reversibility vs. stability,
- anti-capture safeguards vs. speed,
- equal standing vs. expertise dependence.

The purpose of the value tension register is to prevent the fiction that the initial values layer is free of judgment calls.

---

## 15. Bootstrap-Fixed Constitutional Levers

The bootstrap must explicitly mark which constitutional levers are hard-fixed, soft-fixed, and adaptive during the founding phase.

At minimum, the following must be hard-fixed during bootstrap:
- classification criteria for governance and constitutional significance,
- minimum deliberative quorum,
- audit minimum,
- no silent edit rule,
- no active-case retuning rule,
- emergency limits,
- first constitutional review deadline,
- publication duties for founding artifacts.

These levers may later change only through the meta-governance process and never retroactively.

---

## 16. Founding Constraints

The bootstrap phase is subject to the following constraints:

### 16.1 No hidden constitution
The true founding rules may not differ from the published founding rules.

### 16.2 No silent founder privilege
Any founder privilege must be explicitly listed and time-bounded.

### 16.3 No open-ended provisionality
The bootstrap phase may not remain "temporary" indefinitely.

### 16.4 No bootstrap policy laundering
Founders may not smuggle ordinary policy preferences into constitutional startup artifacts unless strictly necessary.

### 16.5 No bootstrap self-ratification shortcut
Founders may not declare the system fully legitimate without a later civic review path.

### 16.6 No bypass of audit publication
Founding changes must be logged from the first meaningful operation.

---

## 17. Bootstrap Legitimacy Claims

The bootstrap may claim only limited legitimacy.

Permissible legitimacy claims during bootstrap are:
- enabling legitimacy,
- protective legitimacy,
- transitional legitimacy,
- disclosed provisional legitimacy.

Impermissible bootstrap claims are:
- full constitutional legitimacy,
- permanent representational legitimacy,
- irreversible constitutional authority,
- immunity from later revision.

---

## 18. First Constitutional Review

The bootstrap phase must define a first constitutional review.

This review is the first moment at which the founding substrate is systematically exposed to the civic loop for challenge, amendment, ratification, narrowing, or rejection.

The first constitutional review must:
- occur on a fixed published schedule,
- be impossible to cancel unilaterally,
- include bootstrap debt review,
- include founder privilege review,
- include classification and registry review,
- include operator trust review,
- include review of who was excluded from the initial public,
- include a decision on which bootstrap exceptions continue, end, or escalate.

A bootstrap without a first constitutional review is a disguised permanent settlement.

---

## 19. Bootstrap Debt Register

The system must maintain a bootstrap debt register.

This register records:
- every founding shortcut,
- every temporary concentration of power,
- every single-provider dependency,
- every unreviewed classification asymmetry,
- every temporarily narrowed membership condition,
- every opaque element tolerated for startup reasons.

Each debt item must include:
- why it exists,
- who authorized it,
- when it expires,
- what review path retires it,
- what happens if it is not retired on time.

---

## 20. Expiry and Transition

The bootstrap phase must end through explicit transition, not drift.

The transition out of bootstrap requires:
- publication of the bootstrap debt status,
- first constitutional review completion,
- epoch transition into post-bootstrap governance,
- confirmation that bootstrap-only privileges are either retired or explicitly re-authorized,
- confirmation that core invariants remained intact during the founding phase.

Bootstrap may not dissolve by silence.

---

## 21. Failed Bootstrap

A bootstrap is considered failed if any of the following occurs:
- the first constitutional review is indefinitely delayed,
- founders retain undeclared control,
- the published framework diverges from real operator behavior,
- emergency or provisional powers become normal runtime behavior,
- the audit trail cannot reconstruct the founding sequence,
- the initial public cannot challenge the bootstrap terms,
- the bootstrap debt register is missing or fictitious.

A failed bootstrap must trigger one of the following:
- freeze and review,
- narrowed continuation under explicit debt escalation,
- restart under revised founding conditions,
- public declaration of invalid bootstrap status.

---

## 22. Bootstrap Restart

If the founding phase is materially compromised, the system may require bootstrap restart.

Bootstrap restart is not ordinary meta-governance.
It is a new constitutional exception justified only when the original founding substrate cannot plausibly support later legitimacy repair.

Restart conditions must be narrowly defined and publicly declared.

---

## 23. Civic Ratification After Bootstrap

The first successful constitutional cycle after bootstrap must determine, explicitly:
- which founding values are retained,
- which founding assumptions are revised,
- which bootstrap debts are retired,
- which transitional mechanisms persist,
- which founder or steward powers are eliminated,
- which unresolved constitutional tensions remain open.

Ratification after bootstrap should be understood not as ceremonial approval, but as the first real test of whether the founding phase has yielded a system that can be governed rather than merely inherited.

---

## 24. Relationship to Recursive Civic Governance

Recursive civic governance begins only after the founding phase creates a minimally valid substrate for self-revision.

The bootstrap therefore precedes recursion, but must be structured so that recursion can later judge it.

The bootstrap is not outside the constitution.
It is the constitution in disclosed provisional form.

---

## 25. Founding Invariant

The system may begin under asymmetry.
It may not hide that asymmetry, normalize it, or render it immune to later civic judgment.

---

## 26. Closing Principle

A constitutional bootstrap is legitimate only when it openly admits that it is not yet fully legitimate.

Its ethical task is not to claim purity.
Its ethical task is to found as little as possible, expose as much as possible, and hand the unfinished constitution to the public as early as possible.
