# Pnyx Trademark Policy

The software and public knowledge in this repository are openly licensed. The **Pnyx** name, project logos, and distinctive visual identity are not licensed for unrestricted branding use.

This policy exists to protect the public from confusion about what is official, endorsed, compatible, or governed by the Pnyx project. It is not intended to prevent truthful discussion, interoperability, criticism, or community participation.

## Project marks

The project marks include:

- the name **Pnyx** when used to identify this civic protocol or its official implementation;
- official Pnyx logos, icons, wordmarks, and visual identity assets;
- confusingly similar names or branding used in the same field.

This policy applies whether or not a particular mark has been formally registered in a jurisdiction.

## Uses that do not require permission

You may use the Pnyx name, without implying endorsement, to:

- refer truthfully to the project, protocol, repository, or community;
- link to or discuss Pnyx in articles, research, reviews, criticism, and educational material;
- state accurately that software is based on, derived from, interoperable with, or compatible with Pnyx;
- identify an unmodified copy of the official project software;
- describe contributions made to the project;
- use phrases such as “for Pnyx”, “compatible with Pnyx”, or “a fork of Pnyx” when the relationship is clear and not misleading.

Reasonable reproduction of the word mark for attribution or screenshots is permitted. These uses must not suggest sponsorship, certification, governance authority, or official status that has not been granted.

## Forks and modified distributions

Open licensing gives you the right to fork and modify covered works. It does not automatically give you the right to present a modified distribution as the official Pnyx project.

A materially modified fork should:

- use a distinct primary name;
- state prominently that it is a fork or derivative of Pnyx;
- identify material modifications;
- avoid official Pnyx logos unless written permission has been granted;
- avoid language that could reasonably imply official governance, certification, or endorsement.

A description such as **“Example Civic, a fork of Pnyx”** is generally acceptable. Naming the modified product simply **“Pnyx”** in a way that creates confusion is not.

## Uses that require permission

Written permission is required to:

- use Pnyx as the primary name of a company, product, hosted service, commercial offering, domain, event, certification, or fundraising campaign;
- use an official Pnyx logo as the primary branding of a modified distribution or third-party service;
- create or sell merchandise using official project marks;
- claim that a product, service, organization, implementation, or person is “official”, “certified”, “approved”, or “endorsed” by Pnyx;
- register a confusingly similar trademark, company name, product name, social-media account, or domain;
- alter an official logo while continuing to present it as an official Pnyx mark.

Permission may include conditions that preserve attribution, prevent confusion, and protect the project's civic mission.

## Community use

Community groups may describe themselves as a **Pnyx community**, **Pnyx study group**, or similar term when they:

- are non-exclusive and open about who organizes them;
- clearly state that they are community-led rather than an official governing body;
- do not imply authority to speak for the project;
- do not use the marks for misleading fundraising or political endorsement.

## No effect on copyright licences

This policy does not reduce rights granted under `EUPL-1.2` or `CC-BY-SA-4.0`. It governs only the use of project marks and claims of affiliation or endorsement.

Nothing here limits uses permitted by applicable trademark law, including legitimate nominative or descriptive use.

## Requests and questions

Until a dedicated trademark contact is published, open a repository issue describing the proposed use unless the request contains confidential information. Confidential requests may be sent privately to the repository owner through the contact information associated with their GitHub profile.

The project may revise this policy publicly as its governance and legal structure mature.

This policy is project guidance and is not legal advice.
